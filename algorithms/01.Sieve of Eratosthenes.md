## Purpose

To find all primes up to n

## Number basics

A number can either be prime or composite.

Prime - A number built from nothing
Composite - Made up of other Prime

## The Algorithm

- Mark everything as prime
- We know the 1st known prime -> 2
- Start marking all the composites made up of 2 up until what we need as non-prime
- Move to next prime and repeat it

We can start from multiple 2 to x where i * prime <= n.
But we can start from i*i, because we would have already seen those numbers.

2 4 6 8 10 12 ...
3 -> if we start i from 2 -> 6 9 12 15 18 ...
3 -> if we start i from x*x -> 9 12 15 18 ...

Why x*x
let's say x is 100.
up to 10000, all composites would have been already marked.
How?
For all numbers from 2 to 99, we would have multiplied by 100 and marked as composite.
200, 300, 400 ... 9900
So we start from 100th multiplier only.

## Time Complexity

markPrime for a prime number takes n/p iterations.
For each prime in n, we do markPrime.

So,
n/p1 + n/p2 + n/p3 ... + n/pn

n (1/p1 + 1/p2 + 1/p3 ... + 1/pn)

Sum of reciprocals of prime:
Merten's theorem -> ~ log log n + B, where B is Mertens constant.

So, T is n * (log log n + B)

T - O(n * log log n)

## **Meaning of “Sieve of Eratosthenes”**

The name comes from the idea of **sieving**—just like you use a physical sieve to remove unwanted
particles and keep the useful ones.

### 🧠 Intuition

Imagine you have all numbers from **2 to N** written down.
Now you want to “sieve out” (remove) the composite numbers and **keep only primes**.

You do this by repeatedly eliminating multiples of primes.

---

### 🪣 Why “sieve”?

Because the algorithm works like shaking a sieve:

* Keep the smallest number left (it must be prime).
* Remove all its multiples (these are composite).
* Repeat for the next remaining number.
* Eventually, only primes remain.

So the numbers “fall through” (get removed) like sand through a sieve, and primes “stay on top.”

---

### 🔍 Step-by-step summary (conceptually)

For numbers `2 … N`:

1. Start from 2 (the first prime).
2. Cross out all multiples of 2.
3. Move to the next uncrossed number, 3.
4. Cross out all multiples of 3.
5. Continue until √N.
6. Whatever remains uncrossed is prime.

---

### 🤓 Why stop at √N?

Because any composite number ≤ N must have at least one factor ≤ √N.
So all composites will eventually be crossed out by the time you reach √N.

---

### 🏁 Final result

A list of all **prime numbers up to N**, obtained by systematically “sieving out” composites.

---

### 🧩 The name

* **Eratosthenes**: a Greek mathematician (3rd century BC).
* **Sieve**: metaphor for filtering numbers.

So literally:
**The method of Eratosthenes for sieving primes from integers.**
