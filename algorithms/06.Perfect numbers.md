## 1. Perfect Numbers

### Definition

A **perfect number** is a **positive integer** that is equal to the **sum of its proper divisors**
(proper divisors = all positive divisors **excluding the number itself**).

### Examples

* **6**
  Proper divisors: 1, 2, 3
  (1 + 2 + 3 = 6) ✅
* **28**
  Proper divisors: 1, 2, 4, 7, 14
  (1 + 2 + 4 + 7 + 14 = 28) ✅

### First Few Perfect Numbers

[
6,; 28,; 496,; 8128,; 33550336,; \dots
]

## 2. Classification of Numbers (Context)

| Type          | Condition                       |
|---------------|---------------------------------|
| **Perfect**   | Sum of proper divisors = number |
| **Abundant**  | Sum > number                    |
| **Deficient** | Sum < number                    |

Example:

* 12 → (1+2+3+4+6=16) → **abundant**
* 8 → (1+2+4=7) → **deficient**

## 3. Mersenne Numbers

### Definition

A **Mersenne number** is a number of the form:
[
M_p = 2^p - 1
]

### Examples

| p  | (2^p - 1)          |
|----|--------------------|
| 2  | 3                  |
| 3  | 7                  |
| 5  | 31                 |
| 7  | 127                |
| 11 | 2047 ❌ (not prime) |

---

## 4. Mersenne Primes

### Definition

A **Mersenne prime** is a Mersenne number that is **prime**.

Important fact:

> If (2^p - 1) is prime, then **p must be prime**,
> but **p being prime does NOT guarantee** (2^p - 1) is prime.

### Examples

| p (prime) | (2^p - 1) | Prime? |
|-----------|-----------|--------|
| 2         | 3         | ✅      |
| 3         | 7         | ✅      |
| 5         | 31        | ✅      |
| 7         | 127       | ✅      |
| 11        | 2047      | ❌      |
| 13        | 8191      | ✅      |

## 5. Euclid’s Theorem (Perfect Numbers – Forward Direction)

### Statement (Euclid, ~300 BC)

If (2^p - 1) is a **prime**, then
[
N = 2^{p-1}(2^p - 1)
]
is a **perfect number**.

### Proof Sketch (Intuition)

* Proper divisors of (N) form a geometric structure
* Their sum equals exactly (N)
* Relies on divisor properties of powers of 2

### Example

Let (p = 3):
[
2^3 - 1 = 7 \text{ (prime)}
]
[
N = 2^{2} \cdot 7 = 28
]
→ **28 is perfect**

---

## 6. Euler’s Theorem (Perfect Numbers – Reverse Direction)

### Statement (Euler, 18th century)

**Every even perfect number** can be written as:
[
N = 2^{p-1}(2^p - 1)
]
where (2^p - 1) is a **Mersenne prime**.

This means:

> **There are no other even perfect numbers** beyond this form.

---

## 7. Euclid–Euler Theorem (Combined)

### Final Theorem

A number is **even and perfect** **if and only if**:
[
N = 2^{p-1}(2^p - 1)
]
where (2^p - 1) is a **Mersenne prime**.

This creates a **one-to-one relationship**:

* Mersenne primes ⇔ Even perfect numbers

---

## 8. Key Observations & Open Problems

### Known Facts

* All **known perfect numbers are even**
* Each known perfect number corresponds to a **Mersenne prime**
* Perfect numbers grow **extremely fast**

### Open Problem (Very Important)

❓ **Does an odd perfect number exist?**

* Unknown to this day
* If one exists, it must be:

    * > (10^{1500})
    * Have at least **9 distinct prime factors**

## 10. Cheat Sheet (1-page summary)

* Perfect number: sum of proper divisors = number

* Euclid: Mersenne prime → perfect number
